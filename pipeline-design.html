Pipeline at a glance

The journey, step by step.

Every client travels six steps — from first contact to a running, improving AI system. Scan this page to see the whole path at once and check it's right. The shape never changes: understand → design → prove small → scale.

0 · Engage→ 1 · Diagnose→ 2 · Design→ 3 · Decide→ 4 · Deploy→ 5 · Drive
0
Engage

"I had a conversation, they clearly understood what I'm dealing with, and I signed up to explore it properly."

The one question

Is this a real client worth a discovery — and do I have enough context to make it efficient?

What the client does

  • Answers the intake chatbot
  • Books a discovery call
  • Signs a discovery agreement

What you do

  • Send the chatbot link
  • Read & verify the qualification doc
  • Decide PROCEED / NEEDS-INFO / DECLINE

You walk away with

  • qualification.md + developer-brief.md
  • Signed discovery agreement
  • Discovery on the calendar
Gate — Qualification doc read & approved, client understands what discovery costs, discovery booked.
1
Diagnose

"They talked to the people who actually do the work and mapped out exactly how my business runs."

The one question

What is actually broken, and what is genuinely worth fixing with AI — and what should we NOT touch?

What the client does

  • Joins discovery call(s)
  • Gives access to a real operator, not just the founder

What you do

  • Map every workflow (trigger → steps → cost)
  • Audit the stack & data flow
  • Rank bottlenecks by hours/$ wasted
  • Classify each: automate / agent / integrate / fix-first / leave

You walk away with

  • workflow-map.md, stack-audit.md
  • opportunity-register.md (ranked)
  • success-metrics.md (numeric, 90-day)
Gate — Operator confirms the map, top 3 opportunities quantified in hours/$, success metrics numeric & time-bound, "cost of doing nothing per month" is answered.
2
Design

"They showed me a clear picture of what they'll build, how it fits my tools, and what it'll be worth — no hand-waving."

The one question

What exactly will we build, and what will it return?

What the client does

  • Picks the top 1–3 opportunities for the first engagement — not all of them

What you do

  • Architecture diagram + data model
  • Agent specs (inputs, tools, approvals, off-switch)
  • Integration plan
  • ROI model using real Phase-1 numbers
  • Risks + milestones

You walk away with

  • design/architecture.md, agents/*.md
  • integrations.md, data-model.md
  • roi-model.md, risks.md, milestones.md
Gate — A second engineer could build it without asking the client; every agent has a human approval point; ROI uses real numbers; every risk has a mitigation.
3
Decide

"I know precisely what I'm getting, what it costs, and why it's worth it. I signed."

The one question

What is in scope, what is the price, and is it closed?

What the client does

  • Reviews the proposal
  • Raises objections live
  • Signs & pays deposit

What you do

  • Scope: in / out / later — explicit
  • Value-based pricing + retainer (never hours)
  • Branded proposal & agreement
  • Proposal review call → close

You walk away with

  • decide/scope.md
  • proposal.pdf + signed agreement.pdf
  • kickoff-plan.md
Gate — Agreement signed, deposit received, kickoff on the calendar within 14 days.
4
Deploy

"It's live, it actually works on my real data, and my own team can run it without calling me."

The one question

Does it work in real life — run by their people, not us?

The client experiences (in order)

  • Kickoff → Build → Pilot
  • Harden → Train → Handoff

What you do

  • Build per design, weekly demos
  • Pilot on real data, human reviews every output
  • Harden edge cases + add off-switch
  • Train the operators, not the founder

You walk away with

  • Working system in client repo
  • runbook.md, sop/*.md, training/
  • acceptance.md (checked & dated), handoff.md
Gate — Every acceptance criterion checked & dated; client ran it solo for 3 business days; off-switches tested; retainer active.
5
Drive

"Every month I get a report showing what it saved me, and they keep bringing the next improvement."

The one question

Is it proving ROI month over month — and what do we build next?

What the client does

  • Joins monthly operating review
  • Joins quarterly business review

What you do (recurring)

  • Monthly report from success-metrics.md
  • Health monitoring + alerts
  • Continuous improvement
  • Expansion → next workflow re-enters at Step 2

You produce each cycle

  • drive/reports/<month>.md
  • incidents/, changelog.md
  • qbr/<quarter>.md
Gate (ongoing) — Report sent on time, SLAs met, ≥1 improvement shipped per month, retainer renewed or expanded.

The 8 rules behind every step

  1. Diagnose before you design. Design before you build. Skipping order is the #1 cause of failed AI projects.
  2. Never automate a broken workflow. Fix the process first, then automate it.
  3. Humans own decisions. Agents own execution. Strategy, pricing, and final approvals stay human.
  4. Everything is written down. Markdown in the client folder is the source of truth.
  5. Pilot before scale. One workflow live and proven beats five promised.
  6. Measure outcomes, not features. Hours saved, error rate, response time — not "we deployed an agent."
  7. Reversibility first. Every automation has an off-switch and a manual fallback for the first 30 days.
  8. Context compounds. Everything learned lives in the client folder so the next step starts from full memory.
