Pipeline at a glance.

Six phases. No phase is skipped. Each phase has a single objective, explicit entry and exit criteria, defined deliverables, a quality gate, and a clear owner. If a gate fails, the engagement returns to the previous phase — it does not advance with open issues.

#PhaseObjectiveOwnerDuration
0 Engage Convert qualified interest into a signed discovery agreement Human + Intake Agent 2–5 days
1 Diagnose Map the business, surface real AI opportunities, validate fit Human-led, agent-supported 1–2 weeks
2 Design Architect the solution, agents, integrations, data model, ROI Solutions Architect + agents 1–2 weeks
3 Decide Scope, price, propose, and close Human-led 3–7 days
4 Deploy Build, integrate, pilot, harden, train, hand off Build agents + human review 3–8 weeks
5 Drive Operate, monitor, optimize, expand Support agents + human strategist Ongoing

Operating principles.

Apply to every phase.

01

Diagnose before you design. Design before you build.

Skipping order is the #1 cause of failed AI projects.

02

Never automate a broken workflow.

Fix the process, then automate it.

03

Humans own decisions. Agents own execution.

Strategy, pricing, hiring, and final approvals stay human.

04

Every deliverable is written down.

Markdown in the client folder is the source of truth.

05

Pilot before scale.

One workflow live and proven beats five workflows promised.

06

Measure outcomes, not features.

Hours saved, error rate, revenue per rep, response time — not "we deployed an agent."

07

Reversibility first.

Every automation has an off-switch and a manual fallback for the first 30 days.

08

Context compounds.

Everything learned lives in clients/<slug>/ so the next phase starts from full memory.

The six phases.

0

Engage

Owner · Human + Intake Agent
Duration · 2–5 days
Turn a qualified prospect into a signed discovery agreement with full context loaded into the system.

Entry criteria

  • Lead has been generated and minimally qualified — budget signal, decision-maker access, fit with the offer.
  • Prospect has agreed to a discovery conversation.

Activities

  1. Send the intake chatbot link.
  2. Chatbot collects: business profile, stack, pain points, desired outcomes, budget signal, timeline, decision-maker access.
  3. Founder reviews qualification.md and developer-brief.md in the client folder.
  4. Verify key claims, decide PROCEED / NEEDS-INFO / DECLINE.
  5. Schedule discovery call. Send pre-call brief.
  6. Sign discovery agreement — paid or scoped free, never open-ended.

Deliverables

  • context/qualification.md
  • context/developer-brief.md
  • context/transcripts/qualification-chat.md
  • Signed discovery agreement (PDF in client folder)

Quality gate — exit criteria

  • Founder has read and approved the qualification doc.
  • Prospect understands what discovery is and what it costs.
  • Discovery date is on the calendar.
1

Diagnose

Owner · Human-led, agent-supported
Duration · 1–2 weeks
Understand the business deeply enough to know what to build, what not to build, and what to fix before building anything.

Entry criteria

  • Phase 0 gate passed.
  • Access to one operator who actually runs the workflows — not only the founder.

Activities

  1. Discovery call(s) — human-led, recorded, transcribed.
  2. Workflow mapping — trigger → steps → tools → owner → frequency → time cost → failure points.
  3. Stack audit — every SaaS tool, where it sits, what data it holds, what's integrated vs. manual.
  4. Bottleneck inventory — what is slow, repetitive, error-prone, or expensive, ranked by hours/$ wasted per month.
  5. Opportunity surfacing — AUTOMATE / AI-AGENT / INTEGRATE / FIX-PROCESS-FIRST / LEAVE-ALONE.
  6. Constraint map — compliance, data residency, internal politics, AI literacy.
  7. Outcome definition — what does success look like in 90 days, in numbers.

Deliverables

  • context/discovery-notes.md
  • context/discovery-transcript.md
  • context/workflow-map.md
  • context/stack-audit.md
  • context/opportunity-register.md
  • context/constraints.md
  • context/success-metrics.md

Quality gate — exit criteria

  • At least one operator has confirmed the workflow map is accurate.
  • Top 3 opportunities are quantified in hours or dollars per month.
  • Success metrics are numeric and time-bound.
  • We can answer: "If we do nothing, what does this cost the business per month?"
2

Design

Owner · Solutions Architect + agents
Duration · 1–2 weeks
Produce architecture, agent specs, integration plan, and ROI case a builder could execute without ambiguity and a decision-maker could approve without hand-waving.

Entry criteria

  • Phase 1 gate passed.
  • Top 1–3 opportunities selected for the initial engagement — not all of them. Sequencing matters.

Activities

  1. Solution architecture — diagram of systems, data flow, agents, humans, checkpoints, external services.
  2. Agent specs — purpose, inputs, outputs, tools, prompts, approval points, success metric, failure handling, off-switch.
  3. Integration plan — per SaaS: API/auth, data direction, sync frequency, error handling, credential owner.
  4. Data model — entities, where they live, system of record.
  5. Process redesign — for workflows being redesigned, document the new process before the build spec.
  6. ROI model — projected hours saved, error reduction, revenue lift, with assumptions stated.
  7. Risk register — what could go wrong, with named mitigation.
  8. Milestone breakdown — phased build with checkpoints, not a single "delivery" date.

Deliverables

  • design/architecture.md
  • design/agents/<agent-name>.md
  • design/integrations.md · data-model.md · process-redesign.md
  • design/roi-model.md · risks.md · milestones.md

Quality gate — exit criteria

  • A second engineer could read the design and build it without asking the client questions.
  • Every agent has a human approval point or a documented justification for autonomous operation.
  • ROI model uses real numbers from Phase 1, not industry averages.
  • Every risk has a named mitigation.
3

Decide

Owner · Human-led
Duration · 3–7 days
Convert the design into a signed agreement at a price that reflects business value, not hours.

Activities

  1. Scope statement — what is in, what is out, what is "later phase." Explicit on all three.
  2. Pricing — value-based, anchored to the ROI model. Retainer for ongoing operation. Never bill hours for AI work.
  3. Proposal — situation → outcome → solution → scope → timeline → investment → support → next step.
  4. Agreement — deliverables, acceptance criteria, payment schedule, IP, off-boarding, retainer terms.
  5. Proposal review call — walk the decision-maker through it. Address objections live.
  6. Close — signature, deposit, kickoff date booked.

Deliverables

  • decide/scope.md
  • decide/proposal.pdf + source .md
  • decide/agreement.pdf (signed)
  • decide/kickoff-plan.md

Quality gate — exit criteria

  • Agreement is signed.
  • Deposit is received.
  • Kickoff is on the calendar within 14 days.
4

Deploy

Owner · Build agents + human review
Duration · 3–8 weeks
Build, ship, and prove one workflow live before expanding to the rest. Hand off a system the client can operate.

Sub-phases

  1. Kickoff — credentials handed off, one comms channel opened, milestone 1 starts.
  2. Build — implement per design/. Daily small commits. Weekly demos.
  3. Pilot — first agent runs against real data with a human reviewing every output. Off-switch live.
  4. Harden — fix edge cases. Add guardrails, rate limits, logging, alerting.
  5. Train — operators trained in their actual workflow, not a slide deck.
  6. Handoff — admin access, documentation, SOPs, support runbook. Retainer kicks in.

Deliverables

  • Working system in the client repo
  • deploy/runbook.md — how to operate, recover, escalate
  • deploy/sop/<workflow>.md — one per automated workflow
  • deploy/training/ — recorded walkthroughs
  • deploy/acceptance.md — every scope item checked, dated, signed
  • deploy/handoff.md — admin access map, vendor accounts, credentials owner

Quality gate — exit criteria

  • All acceptance criteria from decide/scope.md are checked and dated.
  • The client has run the system end-to-end with no help from us for at least 3 business days.
  • Off-switches and manual fallbacks are documented and tested.
  • Retainer is active.
5

Drive

Owner · Support agents + human strategist
Duration · Ongoing
Operate the system, prove the ROI month over month, and expand into the next workflows.

Activities

  1. Monthly operating review — pull metrics from success-metrics.md. Hours saved, error rate, throughput, revenue impact.
  2. Health monitoring — uptime, agent error rates, queue depth, approval latency. Alert before the client notices.
  3. Continuous improvement — fix bugs, tune prompts, refine policies, add edge cases discovered in production.
  4. Expansion — work through opportunity-register.md. Each new workflow re-enters at Phase 2.
  5. Strategic consulting — quarterly business review with the decision-maker.

Recurring deliverables

  • drive/reports/<YYYY-MM>.md
  • drive/incidents/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>.md
  • drive/changelog.md
  • drive/qbr/<YYYY-Qn>.md

Quality gate — ongoing

  • Monthly report sent on time.
  • Promised SLAs met or breach acknowledged with remediation.
  • At least one improvement shipped per month.
  • Retainer renewed or expanded each cycle.

A workflow through the pipeline.

A property management company spends 8 hours per week on cleaner scheduling and follow-ups.

PhaseWhat happens
0 EngageIntake chatbot surfaces "scheduling pain" + tools (Airtable, WhatsApp, Google Cal). Discovery booked.
1 DiagnoseWorkflow map: trigger = booking confirmed; 11 manual steps; 8 hrs/wk; ~15% miss rate. Success metric: <1 hr/wk human time, <2% miss rate.
2 DesignAgent: Scheduling Coordinator. Inputs: Airtable booking. Tools: Airtable, WhatsApp Cloud API, Google Calendar. Human checkpoint: same-day reschedules. Off-switch: pause all dispatches.
3 DecideScope: scheduling agent only (quality scoring → Phase 5 candidate). Price: monthly retainer + setup. Signed.
4 DeployBuild. 2-week pilot with human approving every dispatch. Hardening (timezones, cleaner unavailability). Train ops manager. Handoff.
5 DriveMonthly report: 6.5 hrs/wk saved, 1.2% miss rate. Next workflow from register: supply restock alerts. Re-enters at Phase 2.

The catalog.

The menu of things AI can do for a real business right now. When a client describes their pain, match it to one or more cards below — don't invent a solution from scratch. Each card is a starting recipe that gets refined inside Phase 2 (Design).

How to use this

Read the client's opportunity-register.md. For each top pain point, pick 1–3 cards below that match. Bundle them into the smallest deployable pilot. The rest go in the expansion backlog for Phase 5.

Category 10

Cross-cutting patterns.

The meta-moves that show up in almost every engagement, regardless of industry. Recognize these and you'll spot opportunities faster.

"X to CRM"

Any unstructured input — email, call, voice note, form — becomes structured data in the right system.

"Inbox zero by 9am"

Overnight triage classifies and drafts every reply; founder approves the queue with coffee in hand.

"Voice to action"

Speak a thought into your phone, get a task, a draft, or a handoff created in the right tool.

"Replace the spreadsheet"

A real internal app on top of the data, with role-based access and an audit trail.

"Daily digest"

A morning email that summarizes what would have required logging into six tools.

"Approve in Slack"

Agents pause for human approval inside the chat tool the team already lives in.

"Off-switch dashboard"

One place to pause every agent the moment something looks wrong.

"Searchable archive"

A pile of PDFs, recordings, and emails becomes a queryable Q&A surface.

"Always-on watcher"

Agent monitors something — a competitor, a regulation, a metric — and pings you only when it matters.

"Lead-to-cash thread"

A single thread from first message through signed contract through invoice paid.

"Human in the loop, once"

First 100 runs need approval; after that the agent graduates to autonomous with sampling.

"Two-way sync, one truth"

Pick one system of record per entity. Everything else syncs from it, not to it.

Anti-patterns — never do these.

  • Promise an AI agent before Phase 2 is complete.
  • Quote a price before the ROI model exists.
  • Build before scope is signed.
  • Go live without a pilot.
  • Hand off without operators trained.
  • Ship to production without a changelog and an off-switch.
  • Let a phase exit with an open quality-gate item.
  • Treat the intake chatbot's output as verified truth.

Why this works.

  • It forces the order AI projects actually need: understand → design → prove small → scale.
  • It separates decisions (human) from execution (agent) at every gate.
  • It produces a paper trail that becomes the system's long-term memory.
  • It builds a retainer-shaped relationship by design, not as an afterthought.
  • It is the same six phases for a 2-person business or a 200-person business — only depth changes.
